About me

The path from engineering to product.

Product management wasn’t part of my original plan. I found my way there by following the work I cared about most: listening closely, making sense of complex problems, and turning what I learned into something useful.

I grew up near Delhi, India, and studied Computer Engineering. My first job took me to Mumbai—my first time living entirely on my own and learning how much I could handle without familiar people and places around me. A few years later, I moved to Ecuador for a client assignment. Everything was new: the country, the culture, the people, and the language. I taught myself Spanish, but the most lasting lesson came from working closely with customers. I began to see that behind every technical problem was a human one—and that listening carefully could matter just as much as writing good code.

I later moved to the United States for a master’s degree in computer science. After graduating, I spent five years in engineering and technical leadership at IBM. I still loved building technology, but the questions pulling me forward had changed. Not only “How do we build this?” but “Whose problem are we solving? What should we build, and why?”

That curiosity eventually led me into product management. Today, I’m a Principal Technical Product Manager at Oracle. I haven’t left engineering behind—I still code and stay close to how things are built. Product management has expanded how I use that foundation, helping me connect technology with customer needs, business context, and decisions about what to build next.

Outside of work, my home has slowly filled with plants. A few years ago, I didn’t own a single one; today, I have a collection I propagated myself. I love the quiet ritual of discovering a new root or watching the first leaf unfold—small signs that growth was happening even when I couldn’t see it.

My path has been anything but linear. Each move—across cities, countries, and eventually into product leadership—asked me to begin again, trust myself, and keep following the work that felt right.

You’ll also find me taking long walks, listening to tech podcasts, reading, or getting absorbed in world history. Lately, I’ve also been vibe-coding small apps I can use in everyday life—small, useful things built around problems I understand firsthand.

How I work

The things I come back to.

  1. 01

    Lead with curiosity.

    I start with customer context, product data, and the people closest to the problem. Better direction begins with understanding what matters and why.

  2. 02

    Turn ambiguity into direction.

    I create a clear product point of view, make tradeoffs explicit, and give teams a path they can act on.

  3. 03

    Build alignment that lets teams move.

    I bring product, engineering, design, operations, support, and strategy into the right decisions early—so execution is shared, not dependent on one person.

  4. 04

    Stay close enough to the technology to make better decisions.

    I still code and prototype when it helps me test an idea. Technical depth helps me understand constraints, challenge assumptions, and make stronger product decisions.

  5. 05

    Own the outcome, not just the launch.

    I measure success through adoption, customer value, operational impact, and what we learn next. Quality and trust are part of that outcome.
